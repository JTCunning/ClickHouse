# Rollup columns of TimeSeries samples tables: review, changes, and an A/B design
**Verdict:** worth shipping for instant selectors, which can read a last-sample rollup with exactly the raw result, once two defects are fixed (both fixed on `lilred-perf-rollup-samples`). `rate`, `increase` and `*_over_time` can't use a last-sample rollup without changing their results, so the branch keeps them on raw samples; a rollup that serves them needs a richer column type (question for James below).

**A/B plan:** ab-plans/rollup-samples.md · **PR text:** prs/rollup-samples.md

## What happens (plain language)
With `samples_rollup_steps = '1m, 5m'`, every row of the samples table (one series, one hour) also stores, per step, the last sample of each interval of that step. A range query with a wide step can read that column instead of all raw samples. That is exact for what an instant selector computes (the last sample at or before each evaluation time), and only approximately right for functions that look at every sample of a window.

## Design changes on the branch, and why

1. **Intervals closed on the right: `(k·step, (k+1)·step]` instead of `[k·step, (k+1)·step)`.** PromQL evaluates at multiples of the step and takes the last sample at or before that time. With left-closed intervals a sample exactly at the evaluation time starts the next interval and is replaced by a later one, so the selector returns the previous scrape. Emulated: with scrapes at exact multiples of 15 s (agents with aligned scrapes, synthetic loads) every selector point at 1m, 5m and 1h differed from raw; with Prometheus-style jitter 0.02%. Right-closed: 0 differing points in all shapes. Same change in `timeSeriesGroupArray(<step>)` (merges) and the sink (inserts). Files: `AggregateFunctionTimeSeriesGroupArray.h`, `TimeSeriesSink.cpp`.
2. **The intervals start over in every bucket row.** With right-closed intervals the interval ending at a bucket start spans two rows. The sink now resets per row, so a sample at exactly 3600 s doesn't erase the last sample of the hour before (which would also leave that row's rollup empty and hit the row-count `LOGICAL_ERROR`). Invariant, tested: every row's `samples_<step>` equals `timeSeriesGroupArray(<step>)` of its own raw samples, before and after merges.
3. **One pass over the sorted samples builds every rollup column.** James's idea from the issue. Before: per step a filter pass (a division per sample) plus a whole `makeSamplesBlock` (id, bucket, min/max columns built and thrown away). Now: one walk; each sample is compared with the current interval end of each step, and the end is recomputed (one division) only when a new interval starts.
4. **The rollup step must divide the query step**, not just be ≤ it. A 90s step over a 60s rollup evaluates in the middle of intervals, where the kept sample may be after the evaluation time. `chooseRollupStep()` in `StorageTimeSeriesSelector.cpp`.
5. **PromQL passes the step only for instant selectors evaluated at multiples of it** (start time a multiple of the step, after `offset`, `@` and subqueries, which the range getter already applies). Range selectors always read raw: over a last-sample rollup, `rate(x[5m])` at a 5m step has one sample per window and returns nothing (92–100% of points empty, emulated), `increase(x[1h])` at 1h is ~99% off or empty, `max_over_time` loses spikes (p99 −66% on gauges), and the range selector's stale-marker filter can drop an interval's only kept sample. `timestamp()` goes through the instant-selector path and is exact too. `canReadRollupColumn()` in `fromSelector.cpp`.
6. **A rollup column is chosen before the recent samples table.** Both are written by the same insert, so there's no freshness gap (the "merge delay" concern from the issue doesn't apply to columns). The recent table keeps every sample in 15-minute rows; a 1d query at a 1h step read 96 raw rows per series there, versus about 25 rows with one or two samples each from `samples_3600`. With the old order the 1d window of the A/B could never show a rollup effect.
7. **Docs only in the source.** `time-series.mdx` and `timeSeriesSelector.mdx` are generated (the regions between the AUTOGENERATED markers), and the review bot treats them as read-only: the change belongs in the doc strings, the generator PR updates the pages. Both pages are back as they were at the base. The source got the edits that had been made only in the `.mdx` (the optional-tables sentence, `version = 12` in the example and "currently 12", the settings order); the `.mdx` also had a "rollup samples tables" bullet from the separate-tables design, with a dead anchor, which is gone with the revert.
8. **Small:** `timeSeriesGroupArray(<step>)` rejects a step that overflows the timestamp type (a `UInt32` cast could silently turn it into 0 or a wrong step); its raw path (no step) keeps its own loop, byte-for-byte the master code, so the `samples` column's merges can't slow down.
9. **`CREATE TABLE ... AS` of a rollup table** (found by the preflight review, in the branch's normalization). A copy that dropped a step kept the dropped column's codecs and failed to create its inner table; a copy pinned to version 11 inherited `samples_rollup_steps` and was rejected (the branch's own gtest for this threw). Now the generated rollup columns of the old table are recognized and regenerated for the copy's steps, and the setting isn't copied below version 12. A declared `samples_<n>` for a step not in the setting is an error instead of being dropped silently.
10. **Overflow at the ends of the timestamp range** (also from the review). In the last hour of the `DateTime64(9)` range the start of the next bucket overflows Int64; the new sink loop then never advanced. Bucket membership is now checked as in `makeSamplesBlock`, the interval key is computed from quotient and remainder, and the last interval's end saturates.
11. **`samples_rollup_steps = '1500ms'`** was truncated to one second; steps are parsed in milliseconds now and a fraction of a second is rejected.
12. **Tests and a performance test.** `05621`: the interval convention (positive and negative timestamps), the bucket boundary, the per-row invariant before/after `OPTIMIZE` (also for `DateTime`, `DateTime64(6)`/`Float32`, timestamps before 1970 and the last hour of `DateTime64(9)`), 18 PromQL forms with the column each one reads and result equality with rollups on and off (`offset`, `@`, `timestamp()`, subqueries, an instant query), copies by `CREATE TABLE ... AS`, and the rollup-before-recent order. `05508` looked for `samples_300` with `LIKE`, whose `_` also matches the random UUID of an inner table name (a 1-in-4096 flake): now `position()`. The performance test needs `do_not_check_in_pr="<PR number>"` on its `<create_query>` and `<fill_query>` (master can't create the table), added when the PR exists. `tests/performance/promql_range_rollup_samples.xml`: `sum(m)` at 1h and 5m, rollup vs raw in the same run, `sum(rate(m[5m]))` as the control (2000 series × 1 day; fill 1.6 s and queries ~0.2 s on master here).

## Numbers (emulated, no build)
Emulation: a master binary, rollup columns computed in SQL from hourly rows (both conventions), and the grid functions the converter calls (`timeSeriesLastToGrid` with the 5m lookback for a selector, `timeSeriesRateToGrid`, …). 200 series × 2 days at 15 s, four shapes (exact grid or jittered timestamps; counters with resets or gauges with spikes). `bench/rollup-samples/emulate/emulate.sh`.

| case | rollup | points that differ from raw |
|---|---|---|
| selector, steps 1m / 5m / 1h | right-closed | 0 of 564k / 113k / 9.4k, all shapes |
| selector, steps 1m / 5m / 1h | left-closed (`daa5327`) | 100% on the exact grid; 0.02% jittered |
| `rate(x[5m])`, step 5m | 5m | 92–100% of points empty |
| `rate(x[5m])`, step 5m | 1m | all differ; counters p50 2.5%, p99 15% |
| `rate(x[1h5m])`, step 1h | 1h | counters p50 4.6–5.8%, p99 9.6%; 2% empty |
| `increase(x[1h])`, step 1h | 1h / 5m | ~99% off or empty / counters p50 6.3%, p99 11% |
| `max_over_time(x[1h])`, step 1h | 5m | counters exact; gauges 94% differ, p99 −66% |
| `avg_over_time(x[1h])`, step 1h | 5m | p50 0.5–2.6%, p99 6–21% |

Speed and size: 1000 series × 7 days, jittered gauge, the samples table's codecs (`Delta, T64, ZSTD(3)` / `ALP, ZSTD(3)`), `max_threads=2` on 2 vCPU, median of 5, 0.06 s startup subtracted (`bench/rollup-samples/emulate/time.sh`):

| selector grid over 7 days | raw | rollup | |
|---|---|---|---|
| step 1h (`samples_3600`) | 0.87 s | 0.03 s | ~29× |
| step 5m (`samples_300`) | 1.02 s | 0.20 s | ~5× |
| step 1m (`samples_60`) | 1.26 s | 0.63 s | ~2× |

| column | compressed, % of `samples` |
|---|---|
| `samples_60` | 25.2% |
| `samples_300` | 5.3% |
| `samples_3600` | 0.45% (0.9% with exact-grid timestamps: the bucket-start sample is kept on its own) |

So `'1m, 5m, 1h'` costs about 31% more samples storage and `'5m, 1h'` about 6%. At 15 s scrapes the 1m column saves only 4× reads for its 25% of storage; `'5m, 1h'` is the better default to suggest in the docs.

## Not changed, and why
- **`rate` / `increase` / `*_over_time` on rollups.** Needs a rollup that stores more than the last sample (see the question). Not a small change: a new aggregate state, a second column type, and per-function readers.
- **`ALTER TABLE ... MODIFY SETTING samples_rollup_steps` (still `NOT_IMPLEMENTED`) and backfill.** A possible route: declare each rollup column with `DEFAULT arrayReduce('timeSeriesGroupArray(<step>)', samples.timestamp, samples.value)`, so that `ALTER ... ADD COLUMN` plus `MATERIALIZE COLUMN` backfills old parts with the same values the sink writes (the per-row invariant above). It would also let the sink drop its rollup code. Not done: it changes how inserts fill the column and needs a build to verify.
- **Write amplification.** Measured by the A/B (insert time, written bytes, merge time per inner table). One pass and no throwaway columns should keep it near the cost of compressing the extra columns.
- **The setting name `time_series_prefer_rollup_tables`** still says "tables" although rollups are columns now; unreleased, so renaming is cheap (`..._rollup_columns`). Left to James.
- **The rollup column is preferred to the recent samples table whatever the scrape interval.** With 60 s scrapes and a 1m rollup the column has as many samples as the raw one, and a recent-range query reads the main table instead of the smaller recent table. Likely a small difference (both are read through the primary key); the A/B has an edge run for it.
- **`getRollupStepsSeconds()` parses the setting string on every call** (each query, twice at table creation). Cheap; noted only.

## Question for James
Should rate-type functions get rollups at all? Options: (A, recommended) keep this branch's rule: exact results only, rollups serve selectors and `timestamp()` (dashboards of gauges, `sum by (...) (x)`, subqueries over selectors), and the A/B probe measures what the others would gain. (B) a second column type later, keeping first, last, min, max, sum, count and the samples around counter resets per interval, which makes `rate`, `increase`, `*_over_time` and `resets` exact or near-exact. (C) allow rate-type functions over last-sample rollups behind a setting, documented as approximate (the emulated errors above).
